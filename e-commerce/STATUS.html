Recently completed#

  • Hardening (design/12 gaps closed):
  • Service identity (ADR 0014): order-service and cart-service call inventory, payment, cart and catalog with client-credentials tokens from their own realm clients; each callee checks the audience and the calling client (InternalCallers), and inventory's stock endpoints need the staff role. Tokens are cached and renewed early, so a Keycloak outage is felt only after about 5 minutes (runbook 15.8).
  • Supply chain: one image workflow for every image: an SBOM of the image (Syft) and a vulnerability gate (Trivy); on main a keyless Sigstore signature, the SBOM as a signed attestation and SLSA provenance. Kyverno admits only signed images in staging and production and pins them to the verified digest. Renovate for every ecosystem.
  • Data protection: WAL archive and nightly backups of every PostgreSQL cluster (Barman Cloud plugin, 30 days, point-in-time recovery), restore steps in runbook 15.5, alerts for old backups and stalled archiving; every data store on an encrypted StorageClass in staging and production.
  • Edge: the OWASP Core Rule Set on ModSecurity in front of everything (blocks injection, XSS, traversal, known scanners; the sign-in callback's iss excluded on that path only); Trusted Types enforced in the storefront and every browser journey fails on a CSP violation; payment-service's health and metrics on their own port, so only order-service reaches its API.
  • Tracing in notification-service (Go) and recommendation-service (Python), continuing the checkout's trace through Kafka, RabbitMQ and its retry queues; the edge starts traces; exemplars link latency panels to Tempo.

  • Still open: encryption between pods (mesh or WireGuard) and bot management at a CDN (design/12 §12.8).

  • System walkthrough (design/walkthrough): 11 animated scenarios, 64 narrated steps: architecture tour, browse and search, cart with guest merge, checkout, out of stock, payment declined, payment timeout, cancellation and refund, event fan-out, flash sale, a dependency failing. Every step plays without errors in Chromium.

  • Design documents 11–15: the system and service-interaction map as wired in the code, modules inside a service and communication diagrams of checkout, indexing and cancellation; security (trust boundaries, STRIDE, sign-in, secrets, data protection and its gaps, since closed except encryption between pods and bot management); observability and SLOs; capacity at 1× and 10× with connection and partition budgets (catalog can exhaust its database's connections at full scale-out); runbooks for outbox backlog, DLQ replay, stuck sagas, hot SKUs, database failover, broker loss and cache stampede (product cache entries expire together: no jitter yet).
  • Observability (deploy/observability, ADR 0013): every Java service and the storefront export OpenTelemetry traces through Alloy to Tempo. Outbox rows store the request's traceparent and the relay sends inside it, so one trace shows the request, the gateway, the service, its calls, the event and every consumer (verified: a checkout spans gateway, order, cart, inventory and payment, then order.events consumed by cart and inventory). Logs are ECS JSON collected into Loki by service and level. SLOs are measured per route at the gateway with histogram buckets at each threshold; generate.py produces the recording rules, multi-window burn-rate alerts, the Kubernetes PrometheusRules and three dashboards. Platform alerts (outbox backlog, DLQ, consumer lag, topic growth, payments, rate limiter) have promtool tests and a runbook. In Kubernetes: kube-prometheus-stack, PodMonitors, Strimzi's Kafka exporter, an Alloy DaemonSet routing by trace id to a tail-sampling StatefulSet. Health checks and the relay's polling are excluded from traces.
  • Outbox relay fixed in catalog, inventory, order and payment: it marks what Kafka acknowledged, waits at most 10 s, backs off from 1 s to 30 s after a failure, and exports its backlog and oldest-unpublished age. Before, one failing partition made it re-send the whole batch every 100 ms (444 events became 30 million messages in two hours, and a full disk).

  • Performance tests (tests/performance, Gatling 3.16, Java DSL): browse, search, add to cart and checkout, seeded through the public API, each with a warm-up excluded from the budgets, then held to SPEC §2.2 p99 budgets and compared with a per-environment baseline (> 10 % fails); run-compose.sh with an overlay that lifts per-address limits for a single load generator; nightly CI with the baseline kept in the Actions cache. Locally: product page 9 ms, add to cart 54 ms, place order 320 ms (p99). Search indexes and answers warm requests in about 20 ms, but its simulation needs more than a 4 GB Docker VM (OpenSearch is OOM-killed or starved under load). Without a warm-up the cold JVMs missed two budgets (add to cart 147 ms, place order 1.7 s), which is why every run warms up first.

  • Storefront (web/storefront, design/16, ADR 0012): React Router 8.5 framework mode on Node 26, rendered on the server and acting as backend-for-frontend. Keycloak confidential client with PKCE; tokens only on the server in sessions sealed with AES-256-GCM in redis-session (rotation at sign-in, refresh under a lock, back-channel logout); CSRF by origin plus a per-session token; CSP with a per-page nonce; server-owned checkout idempotency key. Pages for browse, search, product (reviews and recommendations streamed), cart with guest merge, checkout with every outcome, orders with live status and cancellation, reviews, and staff pages (products with If-Match, image upload, categories, stock) behind the staff role and a second factor. Edge routes pages, assets and sign-in (with a per-address limit) to it; compose and k8s bases (HPA, PDB, network policy, External Secrets, realm client) added. 5 browser journeys with axe pass with the 14 API journeys (19 of 19); Lighthouse LCP ≤ 1.0 s, accessibility 100; ZAP baseline 0 failures; 132 kB of JavaScript. Found and fixed on the way: a cached HTML page would share one CSP nonce (HTML is no longer cached), React Router's own origin check failed behind the edge (the edge now forwards the host), duplicate HSTS on assets, API failures surfaced as 500 pages (they now keep their status and a reference id), and catalog had no way to look products up by SKU (GET /products/by-sku).

  • End-to-end tests (tests/e2e, Playwright Test in TypeScript): 17 journeys through the NGINX edge (browse, cart and merge, checkout with every outcome, idempotent replay, cancel, search, reviews, email exactly once, recommendations, analytics); run-compose.sh runs one profile at a time so it fits 4 GB; nightly CI. Core (14), search, async and analytics pass locally; reviews needs more Docker memory than this machine has free. The journeys found and fixed: errors without the promised traceId in every service; invalid input answered with an empty 403 instead of 400 VALIDATION_FAILED (the error page was blocked by security) in every Java service; 401/403 without problem bodies; the checkout rate limit applied to reading, polling and cancelling orders, not only to placing them.

  • Kubernetes overlays (deploy/k8s): dev, staging and prod built from every service's own base; services in e-commerce-shop, operator-managed stores in e-commerce-platform (CloudNativePG + PgBouncer, Strimzi KRaft + the catalogue's topics, Redis per use, OpenSearch, K8ssandra, RabbitMQ with notification's own user, Keycloak with the shop realm); host names set once per environment; dev secrets in Git, External Secrets elsewhere. verify.sh --server sends all 446 objects to a kind API server with strict validation against the pinned CRDs. Found and fixed on the way: the analytics base could not build (dashboard outside its root), the KEDA scaler had no TriggerAuthentication, the edge had no network policy under default deny, and the notification sent log shared an LRU cache (an eviction could send an email twice; it now has redis-notify).

  • Local stack (deploy/compose): every service and store, a Keycloak realm with test users, topics from the event catalogue, profiles so it runs in 4 GB, and smoke.sh buying through the NGINX edge (verified, plus async, search, reviews and analytics profiles). Bringing it up found and fixed: stale upstream addresses in the gateway after a container is replaced, browser-reachable upload URLs in catalog, an optional JWK set URI for the gateway, bash health checks, OpenSearch memory.

  • analytics (ClickHouse 26.8): Kafka engine table with a named collection and error streaming, facts deduplicated by event id, rollups as refreshable views over FINAL so a re-delivered event never doubles revenue (the design's insert-triggered rollup would have), unreadable messages kept with reasons, hashed customer ids, read-only Grafana user, provisioned Grafana 13.2 dashboard whose every query is tested.

  • recommendation-service (Python 3.14, FastAPI): co-purchase counts in Redis sorted sets, with the seen check and every increment in one Lua script so an order counts exactly once (three deliveries and 20 concurrent duplicates count once) and is never marked seen without being counted; top-200 trimming; 503 problem when Redis is down; offsets committed only after counting.

  • notification-service (Go 1.27): router commits Kafka offsets only after RabbitMQ confirms the job; worker with a Redis sent log and claim (three copies of one event → one email, 20 concurrent duplicates → one send), delayed retries through TTL quorum queues (30 s, 2 min, 10 min), 5xx and fifth failures to email.dead, unreadable events to order.events.dlq; distroless image, router and worker deployed separately, workers scaled by queue depth.

  • api-gateway + NGINX edge: routes for every public API (reviews and recommendations before the catalogue route), JWT checks with issuer and audience, request ids end to end, token buckets shared through one Redis Lua script that fall back to a stricter local bucket when Redis does not answer in 50 ms (proved with Redis unreachable), per-route circuit breakers with 503 problem fallbacks; NGINX with TLS, HTTP/2, micro-caching with a cache lock (proved MISS → HIT with one upstream call) and /internal blocked.

  • review-service: Cassandra tables per query with month buckets, one review per customer through a lightweight transaction, counters incremented only by the caller that flips counted (10 concurrent posts by one customer count once; a crash before any step is finished by the retry), verified badge from OrderConfirmed.

  • search-service: OpenSearch read model keyed by SKU with two version guards in one scripted upsert (catalogue and stock), so late, repeated and reordered events are no-ops (proved with stock-before-product, v2-before-v1 and stock 9-before-8); multi-select brand facets, cursor pages with search_after, retry-forever indexing and a dead-letter topic for unreadable events. inventory-service now adds stockVersion to StockChanged.

  • catalog-service: products and categories (PostgreSQL + JSONB), conditional edits with ETag / If-Match (428 without, 412 when stale, proved against a write that commits between the check and the update), Redis cache-aside evicted after commit, presigned uploads straight to S3 storage with confirm-only-if-present, ProductChanged outbox carrying the catalogue version.

  • cart-service: Redis carts with atomic line + expiry writes, guest merge on sign-in, prices re-read from the catalogue with a degraded "pricing pending" mode, checkout snapshot that refuses stale prices, cart emptied once per confirmed order.

  • payment-service: one payment per order, provider calls outside transactions, void requests that always win, reconciler that voids late provider successes (proved with a real late-success race), append-only audit trail.

  • order-service: checkout saga with every compensation path, idempotency keys, crash recovery, monthly partitions with id-based pruning, JWT-secured API, circuit breakers and retries; container tests caught and fixed a millisecond/microsecond mismatch between order ids and partition keys.

  • inventory-service: no-overselling reservations, expiry job, outbox relay, cancellation safety net, REST API with problem details; tests prove 50 concurrent orders for 10 units reserve exactly 10.

  • Walkthrough engine: animated diagrams, light / medium / dark themes, narration on/off with a female voice, automated step check.
  • Repository structure: e-commerce/ self-contained, with design/ (system), services/ (independent projects), deploy/, tests/ and tools/.
