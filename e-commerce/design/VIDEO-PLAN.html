Video plan#

Plans and progress: design plan · implementation plan · status

How the e-commerce platform is taught on video: a set of short videos, one per topic, and one long video that covers the entire system. This document is the plan; nothing in it is built yet.

Files are prepared, never uploaded. The work ends with the video files and one upload sheet per video. Uploading, scheduling and publishing are done by the author, by hand.

1. What is made#

Output Count Length Purpose
Short videos 25 5 to 12 min each One topic each; found by search; watched in playlist order
Long video 1 about 3 h The entire system in one sitting, with chapters

About 26 uploads in all. The short videos are the source of truth: the long video is assembled from them by a script and is never scripted or narrated separately, so a fix is made once and reaches both.

3. The long video#

The E-commerce Platform: Full System Design Course, about 3 hours.

  • Assembled, not recorded: a script joins the final short videos in upload order (0, A1 to A10, B1 to B11, C1 to C3).
  • Each part enters through its body. Every short video is rendered as a full video (opening, credit, closing) and as a part (body only). The long video uses the parts, so the credit and the like-and-subscribe closing appear once, not 25 times.
  • Its own frame: an intro of about 2 minutes (what the course covers, how the system fits together, the three sections), a title card with one spoken line before each part, a section card before each playlist's parts, and a closing summary.
  • Captions: the parts' .srt files are merged, each shifted by its part's start time, together with the captions of the intro, cards and summary.
  • Chapters: one per part, timed from the merged captions; the description lists them, starting at 00:00.
  • Rebuilt whenever a short video changes, then checked again in full: sound, chapter times and captions against the picture.
  • Made last, after every short video is final.

4. How each video is made#

The same pipeline and rules as the security repository (jk-boot-up/security, docs/beginner-plan.md sections 6 and 8, and its video teaching standard):

  • Tooling: videokit (Piper narration, slides, video, captions).
  • Voice: Piper en_US-amy-medium, speed 0.8, −16 LUFS, peaks limited to −1.5 dBFS.
  • Picture: slides laid out at 1920×1080 and encoded at 3840×2160 (H.264, CRF 18, BT.709); readable text sizes.
  • Content: diagrams and walkthrough steps with real values (order IDs, SKUs, amounts, topic names), input and result shown apart, every acronym spelled out, a short tour of the code where it helps.
  • Narration: third-person credit ("This video is presented by Jayasekhar Konduru."); "ID" spoken "I D"; the closing never names the next video.
  • Per video: .mp4, .srt, a 1280×720 thumbnail, and its upload sheet.

5. Upload sheets#

One sheet per video, prepared and never acted on:

  • title (at most 100 characters), description (first sentence names and defines the topic; no http:// or https://; no < or >), tags including the author's name and the repository;
  • chapters timed from the video's own .srt, starting at 00:00, at least three;
  • the playlist and the video's position in it;
  • for the long video, its chapter list; for each short video, a line naming the long video as the full course;
  • settings: Unlisted first, then Public after the quality check; not made for kids; category Science & Technology; English; captions from the .srt.

Sheets are written to docs/youtube.md beside the videos, with one index for the whole set.

6. Order of work#

  1. Before any video: the checks that need more than 4 GB of Docker memory (status, "Resuming on another machine"), so the videos show a system with no open gaps.
  2. Scripts first: write every video's script and review it for technical accuracy against the code and design documents. Mistakes are cheapest here, before anything is rendered.
  3. The overview first, rendered and checked with the author; it fixes the style for the rest.
  4. Playlist A, then B, then C, each video rendered, checked (sound, captions against slides, length, thumbnail) and its sheet written.
  5. The long video last, assembled from the final parts and checked in full.
  6. Hand-over: every file and sheet committed; the author uploads in the order of section 2, then the long video.

7. Done when#

  • 25 short videos and 1 long video, each with .mp4, .srt, thumbnail and upload sheet;
  • every script reviewed for accuracy, every video passing its checks;
  • the long video's chapters and captions match its picture;
  • nothing uploaded, scheduled or published.
